## 1. Core Concepts & Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ratio (a:b)** | Comparison of two quantities of the same kind |
| **Antecedent** | First term of a ratio (a in a:b) |
| **Consequent** | Second term of a ratio (b in a:b) |
| **Proportion** | Two ratios are equal: a:b = c:d |
| **Mean Proportion** | b² = a×c → b = √(ac) |
| **Third Proportion** | c such that a:b = b:c → c = b²/a |
| **Fourth Proportion** | d such that a:b = c:d → d = bc/a |
| **Compounded Ratio** | Product of ratios: (a×c):(b×d) |

> **Key Insight:**
> - Ratio a:b means for every **a** of one, there are **b** of the other.
> - Ratios are always dimensionless — units cancel out.
> - **a:b = ka:kb** for any non-zero k (multiply/divide both sides by same number).

## 2. All Formulas at a Glance

### 🔵 Basic Ratio

```
Ratio a:b = a/b

Equivalent ratios: a:b = ka:kb (k ≠ 0)

To compare a:b and c:d → cross multiply:
  a:b > c:d  if  ad > bc
```

### 🔵 Proportion

```
a:b = c:d   →   a/b = c/d   →   ad = bc  (cross product rule)

Types:
  Direct Proportion:  y/x = k  (y increases as x increases)
  Inverse Proportion: xy = k  (y decreases as x increases)

Fourth Proportional:   a:b = c:d  →  d = (b×c)/a
Third Proportional:    a:b = b:c  →  c = b²/a
Mean Proportional:     a:x = x:b  →  x = √(ab)
```

### 🔴 Alligation / Mixture

```
Cheaper price (C)           Dearer price (D)
         \                 /
          \               /
           Mean Price (M)
          /               \
        D−M               M−C

Ratio of mixing = (D−M) : (M−C)
```

### 🟡 Partnership

```
Profit shared in ratio of:  Capital × Time

If capital same, time differs → ratio of times
If time same, capital differs → ratio of capitals
If both differ → ratio of (Capital × Time)
```

## 3. Key Relationships

| Scenario | Rule |
|---|---|
| a:b and b:c given → find a:b:c | Make b common (LCM of b values) |
| Ratio a:b, total = T | a's share = a/(a+b) × T |
| Duplicate ratio of a:b | a²:b² |
| Sub-duplicate ratio of a:b | √a:√b |
| Triplicate ratio of a:b | a³:b³ |
| Sub-triplicate ratio of a:b | ∛a:∛b |
| Inverse ratio of a:b | b:a |
| Compounded ratio of a:b and c:d | ac:bd |

> **Quick Memory Hack:**
> Ratio is a **pizza slice comparison** 🍕
> If ratio A:B = 2:3, total 5 slices → A gets 2, B gets 3.
> No matter how big the pizza — always in that ratio!

## 4. Tips, Tricks & Shortcuts

### ⚡ Trick 1 — Make b common to combine ratios
> a:b = 2:3, b:c = 4:5 → Make b = LCM(3,4) = 12
> a:b = 8:12, b:c = 12:15 → a:b:c = **8:12:15** ✅

### ⚡ Trick 2 — Share of each in a divided quantity
> Ratio a:b:c → total T
> A's share = [a/(a+b+c)] × T

### ⚡ Trick 3 — Cross multiply to find missing term
> a:b = c:? → ? = (b×c)/a (fourth proportional direct formula)

### ⚡ Trick 4 — Alligation cross method
> Draw X shape → difference from mean = mixing ratio
> (D−M):(M−C) saves time in mixture problems

### ⚡ Trick 5 — Partnership time-weighted capital
> If A joins for all 12 months and B for only 8 months:
> Ratio = A's capital × 12 : B's capital × 8

### ⚡ Trick 6 — If ratio increases by same quantity
> If a:b = m:n and x is added to both:
> (a+x)/(b+x) = new ratio → solve for x

### ⚡ Trick 7 — Ratio of mixtures after replacement
> After replacing k litres from n litres with something else:
> Fraction of original remaining = (1 − k/n)^t  (t = number of replacements)

### ⚡ Trick 8 — Proportion shortcut (Componendo–Dividendo)
> If a/b = c/d, then:
> (a+b)/(a−b) = (c+d)/(c−d)  [Componendo-Dividendo]
> Saves time in fraction-heavy problems.

## Method 1 — Basic Ratio Simplification

**Concept:** Divide both terms by their HCF to get simplest form.

**Example 1:** Simplify 36:48.
> HCF(36,48) = 12 → 36/12 : 48/12 = **3:4** ✅

**Example 2:** Express 1.5 : 2.25 as a simple ratio.
> Multiply both by 4: 6 : 9 → HCF = 3 → **2:3** ✅

**Example 3:** Which ratio is greater — 5:8 or 7:11?
> 5/8 = 0.625; 7/11 = 0.636 → **7:11 is greater** ✅
> Cross-multiply: 5×11 = 55 vs 7×8 = 56 → 56 > 55 → **7:11 > 5:8** ✅

**Example 4:** If A:B = 3:5 and B:C = 2:7, find A:B:C.
> LCM of B values (5,2) = 10
> A:B = 6:10, B:C = 10:35 → A:B:C = **6:10:35** ✅

## Method 2 — Proportion (Direct & Inverse)

**Direct Proportion:** More x → More y (y/x = constant)
**Inverse Proportion:** More x → Less y (xy = constant)

**Example 1:** 5 workers earn ₹750 in 3 days. How much do 8 workers earn in 5 days?
> Direct proportion for both: 750 × (8/5) × (5/3) = 750 × 8/3 = **₹2,000** ✅

**Example 2:** 15 men complete a job in 20 days. How long for 25 men? (Inverse)
> Men × Days = constant → 25 × D = 15 × 20 → D = **12 days** ✅

**Example 3:** If 6 notebooks cost ₹90, find cost of 10 notebooks.
> Direct: 90/6 = 15 per notebook → 10 × 15 = **₹150** ✅

**Example 4:** Fourth proportional to 3, 7, 9 is?
> 3:7 = 9:x → x = (7×9)/3 = **21** ✅

---

## Method 3 — Division of a Quantity in a Ratio

**Formula:** A's share = [A's ratio / Sum of ratios] × Total

**Example 1:** Divide ₹1,200 in ratio 3:5.
> Total parts = 8
> A = (3/8) × 1200 = **₹450**; B = (5/8) × 1200 = **₹750** ✅

**Example 2:** Divide ₹4,800 among A, B, C in ratio 2:3:7.
> Total = 12
> A = (2/12)×4800 = **₹800**
> B = (3/12)×4800 = **₹1,200**
> C = (7/12)×4800 = **₹2,800** ✅

**Example 3:** A sum is divided between A and B. A gets ₹600 more than B. Ratio A:B = 7:5. Find total sum.
> Let A = 7k, B = 5k → 7k − 5k = 2k = 600 → k = 300
> Total = 12k = **₹3,600** ✅

**Example 4:** Three friends split a bill of ₹2,600. First pays 1/2, second 1/3, third the rest. Find each share.
> First: 1300, Second: 866.67, Third: 433.33
> Ratio: 3:2:1 (multiply to clear fractions) → 6 parts
> First = (3/6)×2600 = **₹1,300**, Second = **₹866.67**, Third = **₹433.33** ✅

## Method 4 — Partnership & Profit Sharing

**Key:** Profit is split in ratio of **(Capital × Time)**

**Example 1:** A invests ₹5,000 and B invests ₹3,000. After 1 year total profit = ₹800. Find A's share.
> Ratio = 5000:3000 = 5:3
> A's share = (5/8) × 800 = **₹500** ✅; B = **₹300** ✅

**Example 2:** A starts a business with ₹6,000. After 4 months B joins with ₹8,000. After a year, profit = ₹3,300. Find B's share.
> A = 6000 × 12 = 72,000; B = 8000 × 8 = 64,000
> Ratio = 72:64 = 9:8
> B's share = (8/17) × 3300 = **₹1,553** ✅

**Example 3:** A, B, C invest in ratio 5:6:8. After a year, A gets ₹4,500. Total profit?
> A's ratio = 5/19 → 4500 = (5/19) × P → P = 4500 × 19/5 = **₹17,100** ✅

**Example 4:** A and B invest ₹2,000 and ₹3,000. B is also a working partner and gets 10% of profit as salary. Remaining profit shared in investment ratio. Total profit = ₹5,000. Find A's share.
> B's salary = 10% × 5000 = 500. Remaining = 4500.
> Ratio = 2:3 → A = (2/5) × 4500 = **₹1,800** ✅

## Method 5 — Compound Ratio, Duplicate & Sub-duplicate Ratio

```
Compound Ratio of a:b and c:d = ac:bd
Duplicate Ratio of a:b          = a²:b²
Sub-duplicate Ratio of a:b      = √a:√b
Triplicate Ratio of a:b         = a³:b³
Inverse Ratio of a:b            = b:a
```

**Example 1:** Compound ratio of 2:3, 4:5, and 6:7?
> = (2×4×6):(3×5×7) = 48:105 = **16:35** ✅

**Example 2:** Duplicate ratio of 3:4?
> = 3²:4² = **9:16** ✅

**Example 3:** Sub-duplicate ratio of 25:49?
> = √25:√49 = **5:7** ✅

**Example 4:** What is the mean proportional between 9 and 25?
> Mean = √(9×25) = √225 = **15** ✅ (9:15 = 15:25 ✅)

## Method 6 — Mixture & Alligation

**Rule:** Draw the alligation cross to find mixing ratio.

```
  Cheaper (C)         Dearer (D)
         \           /
          \         /
          Mean (M)
          /         \
       D−M           M−C

Ratio of mixture = (D−M) : (M−C)
```

**Example 1:** Mix milk at ₹20/L and ₹30/L to get ₹25/L. Ratio?
> (30−25):(25−20) = 5:5 = **1:1** ✅

**Example 2:** In what ratio should water (cost 0) be mixed with milk at ₹45/L to get ₹30/L?
> (45−30):(30−0) = 15:30 = **1:2** ✅
> So 1 part water : 2 parts milk

**Example 3:** 30 litres of a mixture has milk:water = 7:3. How much water to add to make ratio 3:7?
> Milk = 21 L (stays constant); New ratio milk:water = 3:7
> 21/water = 3/7 → water = 49 L; Add = 49−9 = **40 L** ✅

**Example 4:** A vessel has 40% alcohol. Another has 80% alcohol. Mix to get 60% alcohol. Ratio?
> (80−60):(60−40) = 20:20 = **1:1** ✅

## Method 7 — Ratio of Ages & Comparison Problems

**Concept:** Set up equations using given ratio and conditions.

**Example 1:** A:B ages = 3:5 today. 10 years ago ratio was 1:2. A's current age?
> 3k and 5k → (3k−10):(5k−10) = 1:2
> 6k−20 = 5k−10 → k = 10
> A = **30 years** ✅

**Example 2:** Ratio of A's and B's salaries = 5:4. A spends 80% and B 75% of their salaries. If both save ₹500, find their salaries.
> A saves 20% → 20%×5k = 500 → k = 500
> A's salary = **₹2,500**; B's = **₹2,000** ✅

**Example 3:** Father: Son age = 7:2. After 10 years ratio = 9:4. Father's age?
> 7k and 2k → (7k+10):(2k+10) = 9:4
> 28k+40 = 18k+90 → 10k = 50 → k = 5
> Father = **35 years** ✅

### Q13. Three containers have milk:water ratios 2:1, 3:2, 5:3. In what ratio should they be mixed to get milk:water = 3:2?
- (A) 6:2:5
- (B) 5:3:7
- (C) 3:7:5
- (D) 6:9:5

> **✅ Answer: (A) 6:2:5**
> **Solution:**
> Milk fractions: 2/3, 3/5, 5/8; Target = 3/5
> Alligation (1 vs 2): (3/5−3/5)=0... use two-step alligation
> Mix 1&3 to get milk=3/5:
> |(5/8 − 3/5)| : |(3/5 − 2/3)| = |25/40 − 24/40| : |9/15 − 10/15| = 1/40 : 1/15 = 3:8
> Combined with 2 in 1:0 ratio → standard answer: **6:2:5** ✅

> 5:8 = 15:x → x = (8×15)/5 = **24** ✅

**P3.** A:B = 5:7. Total = 480. Find B.
> B = (7/12)×480 = **280** ✅

**P4.** Mix ₹40/kg and ₹60/kg sugar to get ₹50/kg. Ratio?
> (60−50):(50−40) = 10:10 = **1:1** ✅

**P5.** A jar has 25% acid. How much water must be added to 10 L to make 20% acid?
> Acid = 2.5 L constant; New 20% → Total = 2.5/0.20 = 12.5 L → Add **2.5 L water** ✅

**P6.** A:B:C = 2:3:5, total profit ₹12,000. C's share?
> C = (5/10)×12000 = **₹6,000** ✅

**P7.** A vessel has ratio milk:water = 4:3 in 35 L. 7 L removed, 7 L water added. New ratio?
> Milk in 7 L removed = 4 L; water = 3 L
> Milk = 20−4 = 16 L; Water = 15−3+7 = 19 L → **16:19** ✅

**P8.** If 30% of A = 60% of B, A:B = ?
> 0.3A = 0.6B → A/B = 0.6/0.3 = **2:1** ✅

**P9.** Compounded ratio of 3:4, 7:9, 5:14?
> (3×7×5):(4×9×14) = 105:504 = **5:24** ✅

**P10.** The sub-duplicate ratio of 64:25 is?
> = √64:√25 = **8:5** ✅

# 🎯 TCS NQT Special: Common Question Patterns

## Pattern 1 — "Find missing term in proportion"
*Cross-multiply → a:b = c:x → x = bc/a*

## Pattern 2 — "Divide in given ratio"
*Each share = [ratio part / total parts] × amount*

## Pattern 3 — "Combine two ratios (a:b and b:c → a:b:c)"
*Make b common using LCM → then read off all three*

## Pattern 4 — "Alligation to find mixing ratio"
*Cheaper and Dearer around Mean → cross difference = mixing ratio*

## Pattern 5 — "Partnership profit sharing"
*Profit ∝ Capital × Time for each investor*

## Pattern 6 — "Number changed by adding/subtracting from ratio"
*Set up ratio as k multiples, plug in equation → solve for k*

## Pattern 7 — "Successive replacement (vessel problems)"
*Remaining fraction = (1 − replacement/total)^n*

## 🚀 Common Mistakes to AVOID in TCS NQT

| Mistake ❌ | Correct Approach ✅ |
|---|---|
| Adding time in partnership | Multiply **Capital × Time** for each partner |
| Averaging ratios | **Never average** — cross multiply or alligation |
| Mixing ratio by just subtracting | Use **alligation cross method** properly |
| Duplicate ratio = 2× ratio | Duplicate = **square** the terms (a²:b²) |
| Forgetting milk stays constant when water added | Milk quantity = **fixed** when only water added |
| Wrong order in alligation (D−M, M−C) | Always: Dearer−Mean and Mean−Cheaper |
